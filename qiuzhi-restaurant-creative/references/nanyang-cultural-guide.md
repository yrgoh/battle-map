# Nanyang Cultural Guide for Copywriting

## 60s Singapore Kampong Culture

### Historical Context
- **Kampong**: Traditional village community, tight-knit, multi-generational
- **Era**: 1960s Singapore - pre-HDB high-rise, shophouse living, communal spaces
- **Values**: Community over individual, family bonds, simplicity, authenticity

### Visual Aesthetic Elements

**Colors:**
- Warm earth tones: terracotta red, mustard yellow, burnt orange
- Natural browns: wood, rattan, bamboo tones
- Cream/off-white backgrounds
- Pops of vintage teal or mint green

**Typography:**
- Hand-painted signboard style
- Retro serif fonts (60s newspaper style)
- Bold condensed fonts for headlines
- Mix English and Chinese characters authentically

**Imagery:**
- Wooden textures (old coffee shop tables, chairs)
- Marble-topped tables with traditional cups
- Charcoal stoves, traditional coffee socks
- Vintage tiles (green/white ceramic patterns)
- Zinc roofs, shophouse architecture
- Old Vespa scooters, rickshaws (subtle background)

### Language & Tone

**Singlish Elements (Use Sparingly):**
- "Shiok" = delicious, fantastic
- "Lah", "Leh", "Lor" = sentence particles (use authentically, not excessively)
- "Can!" = affirmative
- "Jiak" (Hokkien) = eat

**Nostalgic Phrases:**
- "Remember the good old days..."
- "Just like Ah Ma (grandma) used to make"
- "Traditional recipe passed down generations"
- "Authentic taste of old Singapore"
- "Where neighbors become family"

**Emotional Triggers:**
- Family gatherings, multi-generational dining
- Slow mornings, unhurried conversations
- Simple pleasures, honest food
- Community and belonging

## Nanyang Coffee Shop Culture

### Core Menu Items
- **Kopi/Kopi-O**: Traditional coffee (with/without milk)
- **Kaya Toast**: Coconut egg jam on charcoal-grilled bread
- **Soft-boiled eggs**: Runny eggs with soy sauce, pepper
- **Milo Dinosaur**: Iced Milo with extra powder
- **Teh Tarik**: Pulled milk tea

### Cultural Practices
- Ordering coffee: Complex system (kopi, kopi-O, kopi-C, etc.)
- "Chope" culture: Reserve seats with tissue packet
- Communal seating, shared tables
- Uncle/Auntie addressing of staff
- Slow dining, linger for conversation

### Authentic Details
- Coffee "socks" (traditional cloth filters)
- Charcoal stove for toast
- Marble/wooden tables
- Old-school ceiling fans
- Condensed milk in coffee (sweetened)

## Chinese Translation Notes

### Tone in Chinese
- Use 温馨 (warm), 怀旧 (nostalgic), 传统 (traditional) themes
- Family-oriented: 家庭 (family), 团圆 (reunion), 温情 (warmth)
- Authenticity: 正宗 (authentic), 传承 (heritage), 经典 (classic)

### Cultural Keywords
- 南洋 (Nanyang) = Southeast Asia Chinese diaspora
- 咖啡店/咖啡馆 (kopitiam)
- 老字号 (heritage brand/shop)
- 古早味 (nostalgic taste)
- 人情味 (human touch, warmth)

### Avoid
- Overly formal Chinese (too mainland-style)
- Simplified characters can be used, but consider traditional for authentic heritage feel
- Modern slang that breaks nostalgia

## Copywriting Do's and Don'ts

### DO:
✓ Evoke sensory memories (smell of charcoal toast, sound of coffee brewing)
✓ Tell micro-stories (Uncle Lim's 40-year recipe)
✓ Connect past to present (traditional recipe, modern comfort)
✓ Use specific details (1960s recipe, third-generation)
✓ Balance Chinese and English naturally

### DON'T:
✗ Overuse Singlish (becomes gimmicky)
✗ Stereotypical "Oriental" clichés
✗ Too modern/hipster tone (conflicts with heritage)
✗ Generic "grand opening" language
✗ Overly salesy, pushy tone

## Promotion-Specific Angles

### Opening Day
- "New chapter, old traditions"
- "Bringing back the flavors you grew up with"
- "Your new neighborhood gathering place"

### Heritage/Authenticity
- Generational recipes
- Traditional methods (charcoal grilling, hand-pulled coffee)
- Family legacy

### Community Focus
- "Where neighbors meet"
- "Your second home"
- "Building community, one cup at a time"

---

Use this guide as reference, but always prioritize natural, authentic expression over checklist adherence.
